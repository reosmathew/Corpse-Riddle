The Legend

The Hunger That Wears a Corpse

The Vedhal is no mere ghost — he is a cosmic anomaly, a primordial consumer of human consciousness who views the mind of man as a fragile toy. His body hangs in a tomb between two worlds, chained above the roots of the hungry tree, and he does not kill quickly. He targets your sanity.

He feels a twisted amusement watching the sharpest minds of men and women twist, splinter and turn to ash under the torment of his paradoxes. Approach, and he wakes. Wake him, and he asks.

“There is no right or wrong in this chamber — there is only my hunger, and those too weak to escape it.”

He grants you exactly five links to your mortality. Every false answer rips away a fracture of your soul and feeds his ancient hunger. Answer truly, and you will find no gracious host — only an entity irritated to its core, spitting venom before the next descent. Speak clearly into the abyss. If you fall silent, your memory belongs to the tree.

The Ritual

Three Laws of the Tomb

🎙️
Law I

Speak to the Dead

The corpse poses its riddle in a rasping whisper. Answer aloud into the hidden microphone — or type, if your courage fails. Whispers of false choices are also offered.

⛓️
Law II

Five Links to Mortality

Five links bind your soul to the living world. Every wrong answer — every silence — rips one away, feeding his ancient hunger. Lose all five, and your memory belongs to the tree. One candle burns per riddle; when the wax dies, so does a link.

💀
Law III

Failure Has a Sound

A snapped link kills the light: every candle gutters out, the tomb goes black, and his sneaky laugh circles your head — before venomous commentary aimed squarely at your confidence. Survive it, and descend again.
